I have learned most of what I know about support the slow way, by getting things wrong first and remembering how it felt. These are the lessons that stuck, the ones I would hand to myself if I could go back to my first year.

## Write it down

My memory is not as good as I think it is, especially on a busy day. Early on I kept solutions in my head, and they evaporated right when a similar case came back three weeks later. Now I write everything down: what I tried, what worked, what the error actually meant. My notes have saved me more times than I can count, and they have saved teammates too, which is the better reason to write them well.

## Reproduce it before you report it

There is a big difference between "it is broken" and "it breaks when I do these three steps." The first one starts an argument. The second one starts a fix. Learning to reproduce a problem, or to clearly say what I could not reproduce, made my escalations shorter and my working relationships with engineers better. It also taught me how much information hides in the details a user mentions in passing.

## Ask the obvious question

I used to be embarrassed to ask basic questions, like I was supposed to already know. The cost of that pride was time. Now I ask early and plainly, whether it is a customer who knows their setup better than I do or a teammate who solved this last month. Nobody has ever remembered me for asking. They do remember when a case sat for days because I did not.

## The person is not the problem

A frustrated customer is information, not an obstacle. The frustration usually points at the real impact of the issue, and if I listen for it, I understand the case better. Staying calm is not about being polite for its own sake. It is how you hear what actually needs fixing.

None of these lessons are complicated. That is kind of the point. Support rewards the basics done consistently, and I am still working on all of them.
