I did not come to web development through school or a bootcamp. I came to it through my support job. A few years into working technical support, I kept running into the same wall. I could follow a problem through logs and error messages, but the moment an issue touched the actual code of a product, I had to hand it off and wait. I wanted to understand what was on the other side of that handoff.

## Starting from zero

I started with The Odin Project because it was free and because it did not skip the boring parts. It made me set up my own environment, use the command line, and read documentation instead of watching someone else code. The first weeks were mostly HTML and CSS, and honestly, CSS humbled me. I could center a div one day and completely fail at it the next. But something about building a page and seeing it change the moment I saved the file kept me going. It was a different feeling from support work, where you fix things that belong to someone else. This was mine.

## Learning around a full time job

I was learning in the evenings and on days off, in small pieces. Some weeks I made real progress. Some weeks work was busy and I barely opened my laptop at home. What helped was lowering the bar for a study session. Thirty focused minutes counted. Finishing one small exercise counted. I stopped waiting for a free weekend that was never going to come.

## What it changed for me

The biggest change was not any single skill. It was how I read problems. Learning JavaScript taught me to break a big confusing thing into small steps that I could test one at a time. That is the same skill I use on a hard support ticket, I just did not have words for it before. And when I do escalate something now, I write better reports, because I have a rough sense of what an engineer will look for first.

I am not done learning, and I do not think I will ever feel done. But going from curious to capable has been one of the most satisfying things I have done, and it started with deciding that being a beginner again was fine.
