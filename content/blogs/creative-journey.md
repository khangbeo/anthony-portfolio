Before I wrote any code, I drew. Art was my first serious creative outlet, and for years it was the main way I made things. When I started learning web development, I expected it to feel like a completely different world. Parts of it were. But I kept noticing habits from drawing showing up in my code, and they helped more than I expected.

## Seeing before doing

Drawing teaches you to look before you move. You study the shape of something, where the light hits it, how the pieces relate, before you commit to a line. I do the same thing with a bug or a blank page now. I read the whole problem first. I look at the existing code and the structure of the page before I change anything. Rushing in feels productive and usually is not, in art or in code.

## Iteration is the process

Nobody talks about how bad first drafts are in drawing. A sketch is supposed to be rough. You erase, adjust, and redraw until it looks right. Code finally gave me permission to work that way in a technical field. My first version of a layout is a sketch. I expect it to be wrong in specific ways, and each pass gets closer. That mindset took a lot of pressure off when I was starting out, because a broken first attempt stopped feeling like failure.

## Where the eye helps

The visual side transfers most directly to front end work. Spacing, alignment, and color choices are design decisions whether you call them that or not, and years of drawing gave me opinions about them. I care when a page looks cramped or when two elements almost line up but not quite. Those small things are the difference between a page that feels fine and one that feels right.

I still draw, just less often than I used to. But I do not feel like I left that part of myself behind. I traded some of the pencil time for a keyboard, and the creative part came with me.
